RADIO

Two voices on frequency: the controller transmits, the aircraft reads back. Each transmission is spliced from recorded words and run through a VHF air-band chain — band-limited, driven and compressed, with PTT clicks and squelch tails. A spoofed track has no aircraft behind it, so calling one gets you dead air.

THE SCENARIO

Two different threats, and they are not the same problem. One track is a spoofed ADS-B injection — no aircraft exists. One is a real aircraft being hijacked. Calling either one wrong costs you: scramble fighters at a ghost and the real threat flies on unescorted.
👻 GHOST tells — squawk uses an impossible 8/9 (real codes are octal, 0–7) or duplicates another aircraft; its faint primary-radar dot drifts away from the datablock; dead air, always.
🆘 HIJACK tells — primary return correlates perfectly (there is real metal up there) and it answers the radio normally at first. Then a garbled transmission, squawk 7500, NORDO, and a turn for the Strip.
The discriminator is the primary return. Radar skin-paint doesn't care what a transponder claims — if the dot tracks the datablock, the aircraft is real.

READING ALTITUDE

FL090 = 9,000 ft. The datablock prints hundreds of feet, so drop the last two zeros. FL120 = 12,000. FL045 = 4,500. ↑ climbing, ↓ descending, blank = level.
Where the number comes from matters. Altitude is Mode C — it is reported by the aircraft's transponder, not measured by the radar. Primary radar paints the skin and gives you position only. So altitude is the easiest thing on the whole display to fake, and position is the hardest.

SPOOF vs HIJACK — THE RULE

A spoof breaks physics. A hijack breaks the clearance. That one line separates them every time.
👻 Cyber attack / injected track
• Altitude steps 2,000–4,000 ft between sweeps — the tag flashes the jump in red. That implies 40,000+ fpm. A jet climbs ~2,000–3,000 fpm, so it is physically impossible.
• VERT RATE in the target panel reads >40k fpm ⚠.
• Groundspeed twitches by 80+ kt with no altitude or config change.
• PRIMARY reads x.xx NM OFF — the skin paint and the datablock disagree.
• Squawk uses an 8 or 9 (⚠OCTAL on the tag) or duplicates another aircraft. Real Mode A codes are octal: digits 0–7 only.
• Dead air, every time.
🆘 Hijack of a real aircraft
• Every number is physically plausible — smooth 1,800 fpm, speeds that match altitude, no jumps.
• PRIMARY reads CORRELATED. There is real metal up there.
• Squawk is valid — until it becomes 7500.
• It talks to you normally, then goes quiet.
• The anomaly is intent: an unauthorised descent, a turn off the airway, a heading toward something populated.
Why it matters operationally: the ghost wants your attention and your fighters. The hijack wants neither. If you scramble at the impossible-looking track, the plausible-looking one keeps flying at the Strip.
